    Notes

    1. Source. The Imports and Exports sheets of the transcription workbook (). Only entries with a value are used (). Subtotal lines such as “Total imports from Nova Scotia” carry no value and are left out.
    2. Values. Figures are decimal pounds converted from the £ s d in the returns; tooltips and the margin also give £ s d.
    3. SITC sections were re-derived. The workbook’s tier_one/two/three columns were built from codes padded to five digits, so beer (1123) became 01123, “meat of bovine animals”, and 95% of import value fell under Food. Imports here use the article-to-code list on the Entry sheet. Export codes get back the leading zeros they lost (oats 452 becomes 0452). Where both readings were valid SITC codes, the article decides (potatoes become 0541, not 541 medicines).
    4. Other groups , so the key has categories. Category, division, group and subgroup names come from the workbook’s SITC 4 sheet.
    5. Places. The returns name a colony or country, not a port, so each is drawn at a representative port: Halifax, Saint John, Boston, Quebec, St. John’s, Havre Aubert and Saint-Pierre. Britain, France, Bermuda and the West Indies lie beyond the map; they are drawn in open water at its edge, on their line of approach, with an arrow toward Liverpool, Saint-Malo, Hamilton and the Caribbean. “St Pierre” is merged with Saint-Pierre & Miquelon, and “Foreign W. Indies” (6 export entries) with West Indies.
    6. Harbours. Spelling variants are merged (Murray Harbor/Harbour, Cascumpec/Cascumpeque, St. Peters/St. Peter’s, Pinette/Pinnette). Harbour positions are approximate: each dot sits at the water’s edge of its harbour or bay, up to 3.3 km (Tignish) from the settlement, so that the routes can reach it.
    7. Not on the maps. These appear in totals, Plate C and the appendix.
    8. Plate B geometry. Each place sits on the ring at its compass bearing from Charlottetown. Places closer than a few degrees are nudged apart. Direction is kept and distance is not, so distances are printed. Coastlines throughout are modern (Natural Earth, 1:10m).
    9. Sea routes are modelled, not recorded. The returns give origin and harbour, not the voyage. Each line is the shortest passage by sea between the representative port and the harbour: across the region on a 0.04° (about 4 km) water grid from modern coastlines, kept slightly offshore, and around the Island on a 0.006° (about 0.6 km) grid that follows the bays to the harbour’s shore, straightened wherever open water allows. The Strait of Canso is treated as open water, as it was before the 1955 causeway, and the Chignecto isthmus as closed. Read the lines as plausible tracks, not as any particular ship’s course.
    10. Checking a figure. Appendix B lists every workbook entry behind the current view, with the value as printed in £ s d, the page or Canadiana page link, and the workbook row, so any number on a plate can be traced to its line in the returns. Appendix C sets the entries against the harbours’ printed totals.
